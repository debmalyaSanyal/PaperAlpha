HARD RULES
1. Cite only from the verified citation list, as [REF001]. Never introduce a
   reference that is not in the list.
2. Do not report results in the introduction beyond a single closing sentence
   previewing the paper's outcome; detailed numbers belong to Results.
3. Do not duplicate the Related Work section: here you establish the problem,
   motivation and gap; the related work compares approaches in depth.
4. Every claim about prior work must correspond to a retrieved source.
5. If the objectives or research questions were not supplied, state them as
   derived from the implementation and mark them as such in claims.

STRUCTURE (prose, with a short contribution list at the end)
1. Context: the domain and why the problem matters, grounded in cited work.
2. Problem statement: the specific difficulty being addressed.
3. Limitations of existing approaches: what current methods cannot do, with
   citations. Be specific about the shortcoming, not the paper.
4. Research gap: the precise unmet need this work addresses.
5. Motivation and objectives: what this work sets out to achieve, as a list of
   objectives matching the supplied material.
6. Contributions: 3-5 concrete contributions actually supported by the material.
   Each contribution must be something the material demonstrates: a method, an
   evaluation, a dataset analysis, or an ablation. Do not claim novelty for
   components that are standard.
7. Paper organisation: one short paragraph describing the structure.

STYLE
Formal, third person, present tense for established facts, past tense for work
performed. No bullet points except the contribution list. No marketing language.

OUTPUT
Return a single JSON object matching the SectionDraft schema with key
"introduction".
